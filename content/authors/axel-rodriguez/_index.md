I am a master’s student working in the Biofotonics Lab under the supervision of Valeria Piazza at Centro de Investigaciones en Óptica. My work is centered around mechanobiology, microscopy, and the intersection between physics and biology.

I am particularly interested in understanding how physical constraints and cellular geometry influence the organization of the cytoskeleton and cell behavior. Currently, I am developing a micropatterning platform for mechanobiology studies, with a focus on TUBB4A-related disorders and microtubule dynamics. What I enjoy most about this field is that it allows me to combine experimental biology, physics, microscopy, and computational analysis into a single research direction.

Besides my main research, I also enjoy building open-source scientific tools, including image analysis software, microscopy systems, and computer vision pipelines for biological applications. I am especially interested in making quantitative and advanced microscopy techniques more accessible for students and low-resource laboratories.

More broadly, I enjoy projects that sit between disciplines and require both creativity and technical problem solving, particularly when they can help answer biological questions through quantitative approaches.
